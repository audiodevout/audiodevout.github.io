Rendered in Light; Small Cuts

LIGHT INSTALLATION

Small Cuts is a light installation that writes with shadow, and the first version of an ongoing series, Rendered in Light. It returns to the magic lantern, one of the oldest machines for projecting images, and feeds it artwork that began on a screen. Generative drawings made in TouchDesigner are laser-cut and etched into acrylic, turning pixels into physical slides. Loaded into a Kodak Carousel S-AV 2000 slide projector, the slides send the patterns back out as light: enlarged, softened by the lens, and cast across walls, bodies and the room. The work sits between the digital and the analogue. A process that lives in real time on a computer is fixed into a material object, and then released again by the oldest kind of projection, where light simply passes through a cut surface and the image appears in its shadow. Credits Thanks to Maximilian Lana and Marinus Paul for the laser-cutting and slide-making techniques.
